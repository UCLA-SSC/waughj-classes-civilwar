View of the County Prison on the south side of east High Street. On July 2, 1863 Robert E. Lee held a council of war in the building. The third story was added later. The prison is now a Gettysburg municipal building. Erected in 1857, the High Street or "Common" School was the first consolidated public school in Gettysburg. By July 1, 1863 it became a hospital, housing wounded from the battle.
1888 photo of the High Street School. The Presbyterian Church was used as a hospital during and after the battle. Both Presidents Lincoln and Eisenhower attended services in the church. The Presbyterian Church in 1880, as it appeared at the time of the battle.
The birthplace of Mary Virginia "Jennie" Wade on Baltimore Street. Painting of Confederate Sharpshooters who built a barracade across Baltimore St. at Breckinridge St, firing toward Cemetery Hill. Painting by Don Troiani. Same view as painting. West side of Baltimore at Breckinridge Street. The white house on the corner is that of Tillie Pierce, a 15 year old school girl at the time of the battle who wrote a vivid account.
