THE CIVIL WAR ERA IN THE UNITED STATES, 1850-1868

History 147A examines the era of the American Civil War and Reconstruction (1850-1868) with emphasis on the years from 1861-1865. The course combines lectures, readings, slides, handouts, music, website, and films to study the causes of the war, the war itself, and the aftermath, known as "Reconstruction." Students are exposed through multi-media to the sheer drama and tragedy of the conflict. By this method, they appreciate emotionally, and intellectually, exactly how the war affected both ordinary and prominent Americans living in the period. The goal? To educate UCLA students about the vast scope and the long-lived consequences of this bloody conflict in all of its dimensions ­ social, cultural, military, political, and economic.

147A WEBSITE: ADDITIONAL READING ASSIGNMENTS, RESOURCE, AND STUDY GUIDE:

(See "Website Fact Sheet" in packet for description and password) This website was designed specifically, and only, for my 147A students' use as a general source of information and a research tool. Students should be visiting the website constantly. Here is an example of how to use it to your greatest benefit. As you will note, the lecture outlines (in the course packet) have "helpful notes" that are important for that week's lectures. The majority of the first three weeks' notes (or the general explanations for the notes) are found in the "Causes," the "Lincoln," or the "African American" galleries of the website. Say that you did not understand fully a lecture reference to John Brown, and want to find out what you might have missed. Just click on the "Causes" gallery, scroll down the "Biographies" section until you get to "John Brown, Militant Abolitionist," (actually he's the first) and read the short but informative section on his life. My hope is that some students will be inspired to read more widely, but the point is that you can find much of what you need to know about the Civil War and Reconstruction by accessing the 147A website.
