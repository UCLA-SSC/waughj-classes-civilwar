The Siege of Vicksburg
(May 25-July 4, 1863)



Once Maj. Gen. Ulysses S. Grant had navigated the obstacles that blocked his troops' approach to Vicksburg, the Union Army and Navy laid siege to the city, cutting it off from the rest of the world. The Confederates gamely held out for six weeks, but once food and other supplies were exhausted, Confederate Maj. Gen. John C. Pemberton was compelled to surrender. With that surrender, the entire Mississippi River was under Union control and the Confederacy was split into two parts. "The Father of Waters again goes unvexed to the sea," remarked President Lincoln.
