The Fall of Petersburg
(April 2, 1865)



The siege of Petersburg, Va. began on June 9, 1864 and lasted nearly 10 months. Ultimately, Confederate Gen. Robert E. Lee--his army weakened by desertions and a lack of supplies--was forced to make an attempt at breaking through the siege lines. When that failed, Lt. Gen. Ulysses S. Grant ordered a counterattack on April 2 (the Third Battle of Petersburg) that allowed the Union to take possession of the town. This left the Confederate capital at Richmond indefensible (it was evacuated April 2 and fell April 4) and made Robert E. Lee's surrender all but inevitable. His capitulation came one week later, on April 9.
