The Battle of Chancellorsville, Day 2
(May 2, 1863)



On the second day of the Battle of Chancellorsville, Gen. Robert E. Lee and Lt. Gen. Thomas J. "Stonewall" Jackson conspired to take advantage of Union Maj. Gen. Joseph Hooker's timidity. They executed a daring and risky flanking maneuver, surrounding the Northern army on three sides and inflicting huge casualties upon them. It was Jackson's greatest hour, but would also prove to be the end of his military career. Later that evening, while conducting reconnaissance in preparation for the third day of battle, Jackson was struck by friendly fire from Confederate soldiers who mistook him for an enemy patrol. His injuries rendered him unable to perform his duties, and led to his death on May 10.
