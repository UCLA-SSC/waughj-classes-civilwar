Sherman's March to the Sea
(November 15-December 20, 1864)



Following the capture of the city of Atlanta, and armed with a belief that "war is hell," Maj. Gen. William T. Sherman determined to take the Civil War to the Southern populace in hopes of destroying their morale and thus undercutting their support for the war. The first phase of this plan saw Sherman's troops wreaking havoc across Georgia, tearing apart anything they encountered that was deemed to be militarily useful. The march culminated in the fall of the city of Savannah, Ga. on December 22. On that day, Sherman sent President Abraham Lincoln a telegram: "I beg to present you as a Christmas gift the City of Savannah, with one hundred and fifty guns and plenty of ammunition, also about twenty-five thousand bales of cotton."
