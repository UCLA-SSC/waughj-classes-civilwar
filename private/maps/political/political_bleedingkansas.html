Bleeding Kansas, 1856



In 1854, Congress' Kansas-Nebraska Act decreed that the fate of slavery in future states would be determined by popular vote within those states. This policy, called 'popular sovereignty,' set the stage for a series of violent confrontations between pro-slave and anti-slave forces along the Kansas-Missouri Border. The most famous of these was the Pottawatomie Massacre, in which militant abolitionist John Brown (who would later raid Harpers Ferry, Virginia) and his sons slaughtered five people with broadswords.
