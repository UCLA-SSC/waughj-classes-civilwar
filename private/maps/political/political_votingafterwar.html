Voting Patterns After the Civil War, 1876-92



Though the Republican Party of the 1860s had aspirations of building a strong base of support in the postwar South--mostly comprised of former slaves--events made clear that such plans were neither very feasible nor very necessary. The former slave states became a Democratic stronghold after the Civil War, while most of the rest of the nation became reliably Republican. Since the population of the South was much smaller than that of the North and the West, this ushered in a long period of national dominance for the Republican Party; between the election of Abraham Lincoln in 1860 and Franklin Delano Roosevelt in 1932, only two men were elected president as Democrats (Grover Cleveland and Woodrow Wilson).
