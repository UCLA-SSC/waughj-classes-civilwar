Magnus' One Hundred & Fifty Miles
Around Richmond
(1864)



A great many of the maps created during the Civil War were sold to people on the homefront, in order that they might follow the progress of the war (newspapers largely did not have the means to provide accurate, readable maps in a cost-effective manner). As the key battles of 1864 were largely in Virginia--including the Wilderness and Spotsylvania, as the map notes--this was a particularly popular publication. It went through at least a dozen printings, and may have gone through more.
