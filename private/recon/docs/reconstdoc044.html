The Fifteenth Amendment
to the Constitution
by the U.S. Congress

The Fifteenth Amendment to the Constitution was formally adopted on February 3, 1870. The last of the three "Reconstruction Amendments," it declared that a citizen could not be disqualified from voting on the basis of race. In theory, this extended to franchise to African-American men, including former slaves. In practice, the voting rights of African Americans were regularly subverted until the passage of the Voting Rights Act of 1964, nearly a century later.

Section 1. The right of citizens of the United States to vote shall not be denied or abridged by the United States or by any State on account of race, color, or previous condition of servitude.

Section 2. The Congress shall have power to enforce this article by appropriate legislation.

Source: The Constitution of the United States.
