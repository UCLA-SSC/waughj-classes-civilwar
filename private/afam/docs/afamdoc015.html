Advertisements for Runaway Slaves
from the Gazette and Daily Advertiser

For their owners, slaves represented a substantial capital investment--as much as $2,500 per individual--which meant there was considerable financial incentive to recover runaways. A wealthy slave owner could afford the investment in a slave bounty hunter, who would try to track and recover runaways. Less wealthy owners had fewer options, among them placing advertisements in newspapers. The following advertisements, placed in the Gazette and Daily Advertiser of Alexandria, Virginia in the first half of 1820, are characteristic.

1/6/20:

Fifty Dollars Reward. Ran-Away from the subscriber, living near Warrenton, Fauquier county, Va. on the 24th December last, a negro man, named Aaron, aged about 27 years, dark complexion, has a small scar on his face, stammers a little and is very apt when spoken to, to answer some little expression, foreign to the subject. His common clothes are blue and white kersey, has a brown cloth surtout coat and a small fur hat. He sometimes calls himself BOTS, and may perhaps opt that name.

He was seen at the Thoroughfare Mills, in Prince William county, on the 25th ult. Christmas Day, and it is believed he is making for Alexandria, Georgetown or Washington. He may, however, change his route. The above reward of Fifty Dollars will be given for securing him in any jail, so that I may have him again, and all reasonable charges paid if brought home. Enoch Jeffries.

1/15/20:

Fifty Dollars Reward. Ran away from the subscriber on the 1st inst. a negro fellow, named Nelson. He is a bright mulatto, between the age of twenty-five and thirty; five feet nine or ten inches high, hump shouldered, with yellow splotches on each cheek under his eyes, and lisps when talking; he had on a half-worn fur hat, a black cloth coat, Irish linen shirt, and grey cassimere pantaloons, and took with him other clothing. He was raised in Stevensburg, and sold to me by Larkin G. Nall, of that town. It is believed that he has made for Alexandria, with a view to get on in Philadelphia. The above reward will be given for apprehending him, and securing him in any jail so that I get him again, and reasonable charges paid if brought home. Pierce Perry. Near Amisville, at Jefferson Post Office, Culpepper County. January 15.

2/1/20:

Fifty Dollars Reward. Ran Away from the subscriber on the night of the 30th ult. a black man named Thomas Mankins, aged about 20 years, stout made, thick lips, and speaks generally as if something was in his mouth, a very sulky down looking fellow; although he can be smooth and plausible when he wishes to be civil. He had on when he absconded a blue fine cloth coat, close bodied, almost new, and pantaloons and vest of the same colored cloth with other fine clothing not recollected. He can write tolerably well and will probably have a pass forged by himself. The above reward will be given if said negro is caught out of the district, and secured in jail so that I get him again, and 80 dollars if caught within the district, and all reasonable charges paid.

N.B. Said boy has been bred to the Biscuit Baking, and is an expert workman. And'w Jamieson. Febr'y 1.

2/18/20:

Five Dollars Reward. Ran Away from the subscriber a negro boy about 14 years of age, named Green, the property of George Talbot. He had on a blue round about jacket and pantaloons--has a down look--and in his motions slow.

Masters of vessels, and all others, are warned against harboring or conveying away the said boy. Anthony Rhodes. Feb. 18.

2/26/20:

Fifty Dollars Reward. Ran Away, on the 12th inst. from the subscriber, living in the state of Maryland, Charles county, on Mattawoman Swamp, about five miles below Piscataway, a negro man, named Nat; about 5 feet 1O or 11 inches high, about 26 years of age, slender made, has a pleasant countenance when spoken to. He has taken with him several suits of clothing. He will probably endeavor to pass as a free man, as I have reason to believe that he has obtained a free pass. He has a wife living in Prince George's county, near Broad Creek. The above reward will be paid for apprehending and securing the said negro, so that I get him again, and all reasonable charges paid if brought home. William Dixon. Feb. 26.

3/9/20:

Twenty-Five Dollars Reward. Ran Away on Sunday the 5th inst. a negro man named Tom. He had on when he went away a drab broadcloth surtout and dark colored cloth pantaloons, with short boots; but is probably sufficiently in funds to change his clothing if he should think proper to do so. His stature is rather below an ordinary standard, and his features all of the African cast. His age was 34 years of thereabouts. He is uncommonly civil in his deportment, and has been employed as a house servant and waiter for some months past; he has also been employed formerly as an ostler and coachman, and is very fond of horses. The above reward will be given for apprehending him, if he is taken north of the Potomac--and ten dollars if taken in Alexandria or its vicinity. G.A. Brown. Mar. 9.

3/31/20:

Twenty Dollars Reward. Absconded from my service on Wednesday forenoon about 11 o'clock, a mulatto girl named Nanny, about l 8 or 19 years old, about 5 feet 4, or 5 inches high, well set, rather likely in countenance. I will give ten dollars if taken within the District, and secured so that I get her again--and twenty if taken out of the District, with reasonable charges if brought home to me. Isaac Kell. Alexandria, March 31, 1820.

N.B. It is expected she has gone northward. All persons are forewarned not to harbor said girl or employ her at their peril. I.K.

4/8/20:

Fifty Dollars Reward. Ranaway from Parkgate, in Prince William county, on Tuesday, March 28th, where Francis Manuel is overseer, negro Jacob, a small man, about 45 years of age; black with rather a yellow tinge, smartly marked in the face with the small pox; he is a timid negro, has a down look, and rather a whining voice. His clothing, as far as is recollected, was a mixed blue kersey roundabout jacket and twilled cotton and yarn pantaloons, he may have (and it is probable he has) other clothing, as he took off all he had. I will give for him, if taken up within 20 miles and brought home, Ten Dollars; if over 20 miles and under 30, Thirty Dollars, but if over 30 miles, and brought home or secured in jail so that I get him again, Fifty Dollars. All persons are forbid at their peril harboring or entertaining said fellow. Wm. Stuart. April 6.

4/14/20:

Twenty Dollars Reward. Ran Away from the subscriber on Sunday, the 9th inst. a likely mulatto man, named Aleck, who calls himself Alexander Francis, alias Johnson. He is about 21 years of age, 5 feet 8 inches high; was bought of Ric'd. Bland Lee, esq. of this city, and is well known in the District and in Fairfax county, Va.

The above reward will be given for taking up and securing Aleck out of his district; and, if taken and secured within the district, a reward of 10 dollars, will be given. R.C. Weightman. April 14.

5/22/20:

Thirty Dollars Reward. Eloped from the subscriber, living in Richmond county, Va. on Tuesday, the 25th of April, a mulatto man, by the name of Moses, 6 feet high, and about 23 years of age. He has a high forehead, a lounging walk with rather a hoarse voice, a good countenance, and has been accustomed entirely to the house, and looking after horses. He took with him a variety of clothing, but particularly a blue coat of better quality than servants usually wear. There is every reason to believe he means to make his escape to the north. If apprehended within the district of Baltimore, the above reward will be given upon his being secured in jail. Landon Carter, sent near Richmond Court House. May 22.

Source: Daniel Meaders, Advertisements for Runaway Slaves in Virginia, 1801-1820 (New York: Routledge, 1997), 329-331.
