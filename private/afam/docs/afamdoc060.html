Come to Our Relieaf at Home
by Jane Coward and Sarah Reed

Jane Coward was married to Private Pharoah Marshall of the 125th U.S. Colored Infantry. Halfway through the letter, the voice changes from that of Coward to that of Sarah Reed, who apparently had taken in Coward and her three children, and was writing on behalf of the illiterate former slave.

Green Cty Ky. July the 6 1865

Dear Husband i set my self down to write you a few lines to let you know that Mr Reed twoke to me and my three children to live with him to live and R. L. Moor and Mr. Frank Coward come hear to day and beat me nearly to death he says that he will kill any man that will take me in to a house to live with him Pharoah this is roat by Sarah M Reed i want you to hand this to your captain Stranger i want to know of all you that is a friend to the cullard people that you have got thar husband in survice i want you to come to greens burg and treat old Coward just like he did Pharoah wife to day and he said that if i said one word that he would searve me the same way he knocked hear down and old click more hell hear and Coward beat hear nearly to death he took the older girl with him home and he said to jane that before she should live with me he would killer de[ad] and all of the Reeds that was on top of earth in less than one weak i would ceap you wife but R L more says that he will kill every woman that he knows that has got a husband in the army he said that i was no better than a negro rage and i think that i am just as good as he is i never treated nothing as he did Pharoah wife to day i want you to come to greens burg and let me see you and tell you all about it i think we will have to leve hear on the acount off the rebels that is hear for if a man ever leaves his wife and children at home by thar selves they are abusded by some one of them you must do some thing for Mr Frank coward in return to his treatment to day to Pharoah wife i never was so abused in my life by no man my husband is not at home to day i have two children in the uion army and we have two children that was killed in the union army and i think that aught to have some peace at home when my husband leaves me at home do pray do come to our relieaf at home nothing more but this your wife

rote by Sarah M Reed

jane coward

Source: Freedmen and Southern Society Project, Freedom, A Documentary History of Emancipation, 1861-1867 (New York: Cambridge University Press, 1985), 697.
