Request for Clemency
by David Washiton

Often, When trying to get wrongs redressed, Black soldiers faced recalcitrant and even hostile commanding officers. For many, President Abraham Lincoln was the only person above them in the chain of command that they felt certain was sympathetic to their concerns. Here, Private David Washiton applies to the President for help, only to be unsuccessful. At Washiton's general court martial, Captain Andrew Emery testified that the bugler's difficulties began when he failed to appear at assembly. Later, Washiton asked permission to gather witnesses who would prove that he had not been absent, but instead of doing so, he complained to the colonel of the regiment that Emery had tied him up by the thumbs "without cause." Upon learning of Washiton's actions, Emery did tie him up by the thumbs, despite the bugler's resistance and his declaration that he would complain again to the colonel. Within an hour, an unidentified person cut down Washiton, who fled to Vicksburg. The court martial found Washiton guilty of desertion and insubordination and sentenced him to one year's imprisonment at hard labor, with loss of all pay and allowances.

Vicksburg miss. November the 26 1864 Dear Sir i testifie that David Washiton a Bugler of Co B thurd US Colerd Calalry My Capt fell out with me fer what I dont know he struck me I came up to the courthouse and reported him Genl. Slomkum made me stay at the courthouse untill my capt get hear it was too days before my capt came up the capt he then came up frome camps and reported me fer a deserter I was put in geail on the 7'he of August 1864 I am put prson fer twelve month all lowents [allowance] and pay stoped the cort says it can not do me enny good as my cpt reported me as a dezerter and Genal. Slomkem. was not hear at time of my tryl if he had bin hear I wood came clear I am agood solger all ways has done what is right my cap' drew apistole on me iam will to do what right yet my comeny will so to I drop tines fewlines asking fer Jested [justice] I ought not to be in prson if I had Jested done me I am a colerd man I have no edication I dont know nothing at all abought law I am willing to do all I can for you as solger or a man if you pleas doe all you can fer me pleas let me hear from you I am respectfully yours

in Vicksburg miss in prson

David Washiton

Source: Freedmen and Southern Society Project, Freedom, A Documentary History of Emancipation, 1861-1867 (New York: Cambridge University Press, 1985), 182.
