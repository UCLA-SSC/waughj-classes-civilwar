P.G.T. Beauregard
General, C.S. Army
(1818 - 1893)

The controversial Confederate general Pierre Gustave Toutant Beauregard was born in Louisiana on May 28, 1818 to parents of French descent. Beauregard commanded Confederate Armies at many important battles in both the eastern and western theaters of war. He also designed the famous Confederate battle flag.

At the age of sixteen, Beauregard entered the United States Military Academy at West Point. Four years later, he graduated second in his class. Early in his career, Beauregard distinguished himself both in building coastal fortifications along the Gulf Coast and by his service in the Mexican War (1846-48). In 1861, Beauregard returned to West Point to serve as superintendent. His term lasted only five days, however, because President Abraham Lincoln fired him for his secessionist sympathies.

Upon the secession of Louisiana in January of 1861, Beauregard resigned from the Federal Army. Soon after this, Jefferson Davis awarded him a commission as a brigadier general in the Confederate Army. Beauregard assumed command of the forces facing down the Union garrison at Fort Sumter. The surrender of the Union garrison on April 15, 1861, made Beauregard a hero throughout the Confederacy.

Beauregard commanded the Confederate forces in their early victory at Bull Run. As the Confederate army chased the Union army from the field, Beauregard argued unsuccessfully that the Confederates should pursue the enemy all the way back to Washington, D.C. Beauregard lost his popularity when he chastised President Davis in public for not endorsing his pursuit scheme.

In 1862, Beauregard became second in command of the Army of the West under General Albert Sidney Johnston. After Johnson's death during the first day of fighting at Shiloh, Beauregard took command of the army and nearly secured a victory. After the battle, Beauregard turned command of the western army over to Braxton Bragg, and returned to Charleston in hopes of recovering from chronic throat pain. Soon after, Jefferson Davis entrusted Beauregard with the defense of Charleston. Thanks to his able preparations, Beauregard and his men were able to repulse a massive Union siege launched against the city in 1863. In the last year of the Civil War, Beauregard worked under the direction of General Robert E. Lee to protect Richmond, the Confederate capital, from invasion.

Immediately after the Civil War, Beauregard became superintendent of the New Orleans, Jackson, and Great Northern Railroad. From 1879 to 1888, he commanded the Louisiana State Militia, after which he served as commissioner of public works in New Orleans. In his later years, Beauregard engaged in frequent and bitter disputes about the Civil War, especially with Joseph E. Johnston and Jefferson Davis. He died in New Orleans on February 20, 1893.

Source: Joan Waugh, ed., Facts on File Encyclopedia of American History: Civil War and Reconstruction, 1856 to 1869 (New York: Facts on File, 2003), 26-27.
