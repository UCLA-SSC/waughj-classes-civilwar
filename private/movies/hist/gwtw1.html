Gone With the Wind: An Overview

Year of Release: 1939

Cast: Vivien Leigh as Scarlett O'Hara, Hattie McDaniel as Mammy, Butterfly McQueen as Prissy, Leslie Howard as Ashley Wilkes, Olivia de Havilland as Melanie Hamilton, Clark Gable as Rhett Butler

Key People Behind the Scenes: Directed by George Cukor, Victor Fleming, and Sam Wood. Written by Sidney Howard based on the novel by Margaret Mitchell. Produced by David O. Selznick.

Setting: Georgia, in and around the city of Atlanta.

Plot Summary: Southern Belle Scarlett O'Hara loses the boy next door to her cousin, failing to realize that her true love is right in front of her. Meanwhile, the Civil War and the ensuing Reconstruction destroy the O'Hara family's life, forcing Scarlett to rebuild.

Point of View: Very pro-Confederate.

Critics' Response: The majority of critics praised the film's direction, scenic design, and acting, though a few commented on its casual disregard for historical truth. The film continues to regularly land on lists of the top films of all time.

Awards: Nominated for 15 Academy Awards and won 10, including Best Actress, Best Film, Best Director, Best Supporting Actress, and Best Cinematography.

Historians' Response: Historians generally see the film as having much to say about 1930s America, but little to say about the Civil War era. The main issue is that Margaret Mitchell (and to a lesser extent, the makers of the film) were all too happy to embrace the Southern mythology known as the "Lost Cause," particularly as regards African Americans.

Budget: $3,900,000 ($66,660,000 in current dollars).

Box Office Gross: $79,375,000 ($1,301,460,000 in current dollars) in its initial release. Subsequent re-releases over the decades raised the tally to $390,500,000 ($2,179,421,000 in current dollars).

Source: Christopher Bates for the History 139A website
