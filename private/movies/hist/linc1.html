Lincoln: An Overview

Year of Release: 2012

Cast: Daniel Day-Lewis as Abraham Lincoln, Sally Field as Mary Todd Lincoln, David Strathairn as William Seward, Joseph Gordon-Levitt as Robert Lincoln, James Spader as W.N. Bilbo, Hal Holbrook as Preston Blair, Tommy Lee Jones as Thaddeus Stevens, John Hawkes as Robert Latham, Jackie Earle Haley as Alexander Stephens, Bruce McGill as Edwin Stanton.

Key People Behind the Scenes: Directed by Steven Spielberg. Written by Tony Kushner based on Team of Rivals by Doris Kearns Goodwin. Produced by Kathleen Kennedy, Jonathan King, Daniel Lupi, Kristie Macosko, Jeff Skoll, Adam Somner, and Spielberg.

Setting: Washington, D.C. and Virginia.

Plot Summary: President Lincoln presses for passage of a Constitutional amendment outlawing slavery, concerned that such a move will be impossible once the soon-to-surrender Southern states return to the Union.

Point of View: Union, largely very flattering to Lincoln.

Critics' Response: Very enthusiastic, with the performances of Daniel Day-Lewis and Tommy Lee Jones singled out for particular praise.

Awards: More than a dozen major awards, coupled with a dozen Academy Award nominations.

Historians' Response: Generally positive. Most historians observed that some plot details had been exaggerated in order to create drama, but appreciated that Spielberg brought this important story to the big screen, and felt he showed particular skill in capturing the personas of the historical figures portrayed in the movie. Some historians lamented the lack of attention given to African Americans' role in ending slavery.

Budget: $65,000,000.

Box Office Gross: $162,746,805 (as of January 23, 2013).

Source: Christopher Bates for the History 139A website
