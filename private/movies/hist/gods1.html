Gods and Generals: An Overview

Year of Release: 2003

Cast: Jeff Daniels as Lieutenant Colonel Joshua Lawrence Chamberlain, Stephen Lang as Lieutenant General Stonewall Jackson, Robert Duvall as General Robert E. Lee

Key People Behind the Scenes: Directed by Ronald Maxwell. Written by Ronald Maxwell based on the novel by Jeff Shaara. Produced by Moctesuma Estarza, Robert Katz, Mace Neufeld, Robert Rehme, and Ted Turner.

Setting: The eastern theater of the Civil War.

Plot Summary: This movie sets the stage for its sequel Gettysburg, largely by telling the story of Thomas "Stonewall" Jackson from the beginning of the Civil War through his death at the battle of Chancellorsville, a month before Gettysburg.

Point of View: Like Gettysburg, the movie has both Northern and Southern perspectives, but it is even more pro-Southern than its predecessor. So much so, in fact, that one historian has described the movie as the most pro-Confederate film since Birth of a Nation.

Critics' Response: The movie has been almost universally savaged by critics for its slow pacing, its excessive length, its poorly written dialogue, and its racial politics.

Awards: None.

Historians' Response: While conceding its accuracy in terms of details, the movie has been roundly derided for its pro-Confederate perspective.

Budget: $56,000,000 ($68,002,000 in current dollars).

Box Office Gross: $12,875,000 ($13,711,000 in current dollars).

Source: Christopher Bates for the History 139A website
