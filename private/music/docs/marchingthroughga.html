Marching Through Georgia
by Henry Clay Work

General William T. Sherman's devastating and controversial march to the sea in 1864 prompted Northern composers to produce a battery of songs celebrating the general and his troops and projecting the war's end--none better known than Henry Clay Work's "Marching through Georgia." In the South, Sherman's "scorched earth" policy caused bitterness for generations, and many Southerners associated the defeat with the song. Even today, a performance of "Marching Through Georgia" would not be well-received.

Bring the good ol' Bugle boys! We'll sing another song,
Sing it with a spirit that will start the world along,
Sing it like we used to sing to sing it fifty thousand strong,
While we were marching through Georgia

CHORUS: Hurrah! Hurrah! We bring the Jubilee.
Hurrah! Hurrah! The flag that makes you free,
So we sang the chorus from Atlanta to the sea,
While we were marching through Georgia. (Repeat)

Source (Original Song): Henry Clay Work, "Marching through Georgia" (Cleveland: S. Brainard's Sons, 1865).

Source (Audio Recording): Jay Unger and Molly Mason, Songs of the Civil War (Los Angeles: Sony Music Entertainment, 1991).
