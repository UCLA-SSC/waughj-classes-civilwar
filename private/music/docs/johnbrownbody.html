John Brown's Body
by C.S. Marsh

"John Brown's Body" was, perhaps, the favorite marching song of the Union Army. Known originally as "The John Brown Song," the lyrics were penned early in the Civil War. The music was much older, having been used by Methodists at their camp meetings at least as early as 1800. Later in 1861, Julia Ward Howe wrote different lyrics for the tune, and so created the "Battle Hymn of the Republic." As the "Battle Hymn" and "John Brown's Body" have the same music and similar themes, it was common for Northerners to mix the two songs together while singing them, as with this selection.

Mine eyes have seen the glory of the coming of the Lord;
He is trampling out the vintage where grapes of wrath are stored;
He hath loosed the fateful lightning of His terrible swift sword,
His truth is marching on.

CHORUS: Glory, Glory! Hallelujah!
Glory, Glory! Hallelujah!
Glory, Glory! Hallelujah!
His truth is marching on

I have seen Him in the watchfires of a hundred circling camps;
They have built Him an altar in the evening dews and damps;
I can read His righteous sentence by the dim and flaring lamps,
His day is marching on.--CHORUS

John Brown's body lies a-mold'ring in its grave
John Brown's body lies a-mold'ring in its grave
John Brown's body lies a-mold'ring in its grave
But his soul goes marching on--CHORUS

He has gone to be a soldier in the army of the Lord;
He has gone to be a soldier in the army of the Lord;
He has gone to be a soldier in the army of the Lord;
His soul goes marching on--CHORUS

He captured Harper's Ferry with his nineteen men so true
He frightened old Virginia till she trembled through and through
They hung him for a traitor, themselves the traitor crew
His soul is marching on--CHORUS
