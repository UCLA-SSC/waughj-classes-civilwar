Stonewall Jackson's Way
by John Williamson Palmer

Though composers during the war crafted numerous songs celebrating well-known generals (from both sides), relatively few of these efforts survived much beyond the war. An exception is "Stonewall Jackson's Way," first published in 1862. The original sheet music bore the notation that the song was "found on a Confederate sergeant of the old Stonewall brigade taken at Winchester, Virginia," but modern research has yielded an even stranger story. One of the Northern newspaper reporters at the battle of Antietam, John Williamson Palmer, wrote the words to the song during the battle, but the paper fell from his pocket and was found by the Confederate sergeant, who was then killed. The poem was published in a Baltimore newspaper, attached to a familiar tune, and was soon being sung throughout the city by Southern sympathizers. As with several other pro-Southern songs--notably "The Bonnie Blue Flag"--the head of the Union occupational force tried to ban the song and confiscated as many copies of the sheet music as he could lay his hands on. By then it was too late--the song had made its way south, where it attained new popularity as a result of Jackson's death after the Battle of Chancellorsville six months later.

Come, stack arms, men! Pile on the rails,
Stir up the camp-fire bright;
No matter if the canteen fails,
We'll make a roaring night.
Here Shenandoah crawls along,
Here burly Blue Ridge echoes strong,
To swell the brigade's rousing song
Of "Stonewall Jackson's way."

We see him now, -- the old slouched hat
Couched o'er his eye askew;
The shrewd, dry smile, the speech so pat,
So calm, so blunt, so true.
The "Blue-Light Elder" knows 'em well;
Says he, "That's Banks, -- he's fond of shell;
Lord save his soul! we'll give him hell,
That's "Stonewall Jackson's way."

He's in the saddle now. Fall in!
Steady! the whole brigade!
Hill's at the ford cut off; we'll win
His way out, ball and blade!
What matter if our shoes are worn?
What matter if our feet are torn?
"Quick-step! we're with him 'ere the dawn!"
That's "Stonewall Jackson's way."

Silence! ground arms! kneel all! caps off!
Old "Blue Light's" going to pray.
Strangle the fool that dares to scoff!
Attention! it's his way.
Appealing from his native sod,
"Hear us, Almighty God,
Lay bare Thine arm; stretch forth Thy rod!"
Amen! That's "Stonewall Jackson's way."

Ah! Maiden, wait and watch and yearn
For news of Stonewall's band!
Ah! Widow, read, with eyes that burn,
That ring upon thy hand.
Ah! Wife, sew on, hope on and pray;
Thy life shall not be all forlorn;
The foe had better ne'er been born
That gets in "Stonewall's way."

Source (Original Song): John Williamson Palmer, "Stonewall Jackson's Way" (1862: Miller & Beacham, 1862).

Source (Audio Recording): Keith and Rusty McNeil, Civil War Songs (Riverside, CA: WEM Records, 1990).
