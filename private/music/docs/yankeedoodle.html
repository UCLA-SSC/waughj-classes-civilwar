Yankee Doodle
by Anonymous

The origins of "Yankee Doodle," which date back to the Seven Years' War of the 1760s, are unclear. The song was apparently sung by British soldiers--who were poking fun at their American counterparts--and then was embraced thereafter as a patriotic anthem by the United States. It remained a popular tune at the time of the Civil War.

O, Yankee Doodle went to town
Riding on a pony,
Stuck a feather in his cap
And called it 'macaroni.'

Yankee Doodle keep it up,
Yankee Doodle dandy,
Mind the music and the step,
And with the girls be handy. (x6)

Source (Audio Recording): Pete Seeger, American Folk, Game and Activity Songs for Children (Washington, DC: Smithsonian Folkways, 2000).
