The Vacant Chair
by Henry J. Washburn and George F. Root

Written at Christmas time in 1861, this maudlin bit of verse undoubtedly struck a responsive note in many a home, both North and South, where the family gathered for the annual celebration and found themselves staring at the "vacant chair." The poem first appeared in the Worcester Spy, and was written in commemoration of the death of Lieutenant John William Grout, Fifteenth Massachusetts Volunteer Infantry. In 1863, George F. Root set the poem to music, and it was a smash hit. Soldier and civilian alike responded to the unabashedly emotional tone of the composition and made it one of the most widely sung songs of the war.

We shall meet but we shall miss him.
There will be one vacant chair.
We shall linger to caress him
When we breathe our ev'nin prayer.
When a year ago we gathered,
Joy was in his mild blue eye.
Now the golden cord is severed,
And our hopes in ruin lie.

CHORUS: We shall meet, but we shall miss him.
There will be one vacant chair.
We shall linger to caress him
When we breathe our ev'nin prayer.

At our fireside, sad and lonely,
Often will the bosom swell
At remembrance of the story
How our noble Willie fell.
How he strove to bear our banner
Thro' the thickest of the fight
And uphold our country's honor
In the strength of manhood's might.--CHORUS

True, they tell us wreaths of glory
Evermore will deck his brow,
But this soothes the anguish only,
Sweeping o'er our heartstrings now.
Sleep today, O early fallen,
In thy green and narrow bed.
Dirges from the pine and cypress
Mingle with the tears we shed.--CHORUS

Source (Original Song): Henry J. Washburn and George F. Root, "The Vacant Chair" (Chicago: Root & Cady, 1863).
