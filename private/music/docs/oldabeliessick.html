Old Abe Lies Sick
by Anonymous

"Old Abe Lies Sick" is a Southern parody of the popular folk tune "The Girl I Left Behind Me." Such parodies--with their very pointed messages--were commonplace on both sides of the Civil War. Nothing is known of the author of the original tune nor the adaptation. The reference to Lincoln's illness suggests the song must have been created in the latter part of 1863, when Lincoln was left bedridden with smallpox.

Old Abe lies sick, Old Abe lies sick
Old Abe lies sick in bed
He's a lying dog, he's a crying dog
With murder in his head

Old Abe lies sick, Old Abe lies sick
Old Abe lies sick in bed
He's a lying dog, he's a crying dog
With murder in his head
Jeff Davis rides a big fine horse
Abe Lincoln rides a mule

Jeff Davis is a gentleman
Abe Lincoln is a fool
Jeff Davis rides a big fine horse
Abe Lincoln rides a mule

Jeff Davis is a gentleman
Abe Lincoln is a fool

Source (Audio Recording): Bobby Horton, Homespun Songs of the Confederate Army Vol. 2 (Birmingham, AL: Bobby Horton, 2008).
