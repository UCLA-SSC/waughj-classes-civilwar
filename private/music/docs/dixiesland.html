Dixie
by Daniel Decatur Emmett

"Dixie"--originally published under the title "I Wish I Was in Dixie's Land"--was written in 1859 by Daniel Decatur Emmett, one of the nation's most popular entertainers. It was a smashing nationwide success for two years, but fell out of favor in the North once the Confederacy adopted the song as an unofficial anthem. Emmett was horrified by this development. "If I'd known to what use they were going to put my song," he remarked, "I'll be damned if I'd have written it!" Southerners adapted the lyrics of the song--originally a comic composition--to something more apropos to their needs, as shown here. At the end of the war, Abraham Lincoln asked a band to perform the song. "I have always thought Dixie one of the best tunes I ever heard," he explained. "Our adversaries over the way attempted to appropriate it, but we have fairly captured it."
