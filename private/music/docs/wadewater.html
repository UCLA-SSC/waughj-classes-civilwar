Wade in the Water
by Anonymous

Slaves often sang, at least when they were permitted to do so. Among the most popular slave spirituals was "Wade in the Water," which took the form shown here in the early 1830s. The song serves as an excellent illustration of why slave owners were sometimes leery of permitting their bondsmen to sing. To start, its reference to Jesus' baptism in the Jordan and the Israelites' escape from Egypt hint at an imminent redemption, and perhaps even an escape from slavery. Further, many slave owners feared that the song contained hidden advice to slaves--that they could hide their tracks and escape slave catchers by crossing over bodies of water.
