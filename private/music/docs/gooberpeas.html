Goober Peas
by A. Pindar and P. Nutt

"Goober Peas" emerged from the ranks of the Confederate Army in the second half of the Civil War, when supplies were scarce and troops had to live off the land. The song was already several years old when it was first published in 1866, under the obviously invented names of A. Pindar (Greek for 'a poet') and P. Nutt (a pun on 'peanut').

Sitting by the roadside on a summer day
Chatting with my mess-mates, passin' time away
Laying in the shadows underneath the trees
Goodness, how delicious, eatin' goober peas.

CHORUS: Peas, peas, peas, peas
Eatin' goober peas
Goodness, how delicious,
Eatin' goober peas.

When a horse-man passes, the soldiers have a rule
They cry out ay their loudest, "Hey mister, here's your mule!"
But another pleasure, enchanting-er than these
Is wearing out your grinders, eating goober peas.--CHORUS

Well just before the battle, the General heard a row
He says "The Yanks are coming, I hear their rifles now."
He turned around in wonder, and what d'ya think he sees?
The Georgia Militia eatin' goober peas.--CHORUS

I think my song has lasted almost long enough.
The subject interesting, but the rhymes are gettin' tough.
I wish this war was over, and free from rags and fleas
We'd kiss our wives and sweethearts, say goo-bye goober peas.--CHORUS

Source (Original Song): A. Pindar and P. Nutt, "Goober Peas" (New Orleans: A.E. Blackmar, 1866).

Source (Audio Recording): Great American String Band, 200 Years of American Heritage in Song (Los Angeles: CMH Records, 1991).
