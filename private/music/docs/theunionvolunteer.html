The Union Volunteer
by Anonymous

The origin of most Northern songs from the Civil War is known, but such is not the case with "The Union Volunteer." The tone of the song, as well as its focus on voluntarism--an increasingly contentious issue as the war dragged on--indicates a composition date of 1861.

CHORUS: Johnny is my darling, my darling, my darling,
Johnny is my darling, the Union volunteer.

'Twas on the sunny morning,
The brightest of the year,
When Johnny come to my town,
A Union volunteer.--CHORUS

As he came marching up the street,
The bands played loud and clear;
And everyone came out to greet
The Union volunteer.--CHORUS

With proudly waving starry flags
and hearts that knew no fear;
He came to fight for freedom's rights,
A Union volunteer.--CHORUS

But though he's gone to glory win,
And I left lonely here,
He'll soon return to me again
As Cupid's volunteer.--CHORUS (x2)

Source (Audio Recording): Bobby Horton, Homespun Songs of the Union Army Vol. 1 (Birmingham, AL: Bobby Horton, 1987).
