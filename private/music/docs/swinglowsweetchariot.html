Swing Low, Sweet Chariot
by Wallis Willis

"Swing Low, Sweet Chariot" is the most famous of all slave spirituals--in part because it was used by activists during the Civil Rights Movement, and in part because it has been recorded more than 100 times by different artists. Unlike most slave songs, the author of "Swing Low, Sweet Chariot" is known: the tune is the work of Wallace Willis, a Choctaw Indian who composed a number of spirituals. As with other notable slave spirituals, "Swing Low, Sweet Chariot" has a message of redemption--that a rescue from a life of oppression is coming, either in this life or the next.

CHORUS: Swing low, sweet chariot
Coming for to carry me home,
Swing low, sweet chariot,
Coming for to carry me home.

I looked over Jordan, and what did I see
Coming for to carry me home?
A band of angels coming after me,
Coming for to carry me home.--CHORUS

If you get there before I do,
(Coming for to carry me home)
Tell all my friends I'm coming too.
(Coming for to carry me home)--CHORUS

I'm on the upward heavenly way,
(Coming for to carry me home)
Since Jesus washed my sins away.
(Coming for to carry me home)--CHORUS (x3)

Source (Original Song): Wallis Willis, "Swing Low, Sweet Chariot" (1862).
