The Bonnie Blue Flag
by Harry Macarthy

Next to "Dixie," the best known of all of the Confederate songs was "The Bonnie Blue Flag." The lyrics--set to an old Irish tune--were inspired by the flag used by South Carolina after the state withdrew from the Union in December 1860: plain blue with a single white star in the center. Since no official Confederate flag had as yet been adopted, South Carolina's became the temporary banner for the Confederacy while the other states of the South debated secession (An official Confederate flag was adopted in January 1861.) Over time, the song was revised to accomodate the addition of new states to the Confederacy.

We are a band of brothers
And native to the soil,
Fighting for the property
We gained by honest toil;
And when our rights were threatened,
The cry rose near and far--
"Hurrah for the Bonnie Blue Flag
That bears a single star!"

CHORUS: Hurrah! Hurrah!
For Southern rights hurrah!
Hurrah for the Bonnie Blue Flag
That carries a single star.

As long as the Union
Was faithful to her trust,
Like friends and like brothers
We were kind and we were just;
But now, when Northern treachery
Attempts our rights to mar,
We hoist on high the Bonnie Blue Flag
That bears a single star.--CHORUS

Source (Original Song): Harry Macarthy, "The Bonnie Blue Flag" (New Orleans: A.E. Blackmar and Brothers, 1861).
