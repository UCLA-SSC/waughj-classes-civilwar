We Have Had a Hard Fight
by Issac Gardner

Issac Gardner was a cannonier with the Ohio Light Artillery.

July 5, 1863

Dear Parents,

There is a man that is going from here toward the North somewhere who says he will take letters, so I will try to scratch a few lines. I have not more than 5 minutes to write in.

We have had a hard fight here and come off victorious. I suppose it will be one of the largest battles of the war. We have killed them by hundreds and by thousands, and have taken no end of prisoners, but have lost ourselves comparatively little. Our Battery has been engaged 3 different days and have lost quite a number wounded, although most of them will be well in a few weeks. I came off all right as usual. Charley Munroe was wounded the first day's fight, and I have not seen him since, I suppose he is in the hospital here somewhere, he is not wounded serious, just a flesh wound in the side as near as I could learn.

I was No. 1 and was tending to the vent of our gun when a shot struck the trail - just missing my legs, and cut it nearly off. Jim Austin, who is our gunner, was hurt by the trail flying against him when it was struck, and he is rather sore and lame but he is around us yet. John B. is all right. Sutcliff from Ravenna was bruised by a cannon shot striking under his foot and tearing the sole of his boot off, but did not cut his foot although it bruised him considerable.

I must close now, I will try to write more the next time,

Your son Issac

Source: Henry Steele Commager, ed., The Blue and the Gray: The Story of the Civil War as Told by Participants (New York: Fairfax Press, 1982), 549-550.
