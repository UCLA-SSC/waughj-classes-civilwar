Desiccated Vegetables
by Robert H. Strong

Union soldier Robert H. Strong had vivid memories of the strange concoction distributed to Northern troops to keep them from getting scurvy. This account was written in 1872.

At times we drew what was called desiccated vegetables. This was a composition of everything in the vegetable world--peas, beans, cabbage, turnips, carrots, peppers, onions, beets, radishes, parsnips, parsley--with grass to hold it all together. This mixture was dried and pressed into pieces an inch square. One of these pieces, put in water, would swell and swell, then swell some more, until no one man could eat it all. We made soup of it. It doesn't sound very palatable, but after a regular diet of hardtack, a change to vegetable soup was not to be despised.

Source: Robert H. Strong, A Yankee Private's Civil War (Chicago: H. Regnery Co., 1961), 104.
