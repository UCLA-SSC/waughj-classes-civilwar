Week 6 Handout: Gettysburg, Day 2

The situation at the end of Day 2

4:00 p.m. to dusk: Confederates attack lightly defended Union left; Confederates take Devil's Den but are held off at the base of Little Round Top

After Devil's Den to dusk: The Wheat Field changes hands four times. Sickles' Union III Corps are finally routed to the base of Little Round Top, where continued Confederate assault is held off.

6:30 p.m. until after dark: Ewell's Confederate divisions gain some ground, but fail to take Union positions on Culp's and Cemetery Hill

On the evening of July 2, Jubal Early's confederates attacked the Union defensive line on Cemetery Hill, overlooking the town of Gettysburg. The rebels experienced severe losses in their abortive assault.
The flamboyant Daniel Sickles anchored the left of his advance line at Devil's Den, a rocky terrain exposed to attack on three sides. The successful Confederate advance almost turned the Union left flank.
Confederate division commander John Bell Hood, whose troops were involved in the assault upon Little Round Top. Hood was incapacitated early in the fighting of July 2, by a shot which shattered his left arm.
Joshua Lawrence Chamberlain, leader of the 20th Maine, and one of the heroes of Gettysburg
In the late afternoon of July 2, a Confederate Brigade was poised to capture the undefended Little Round Top, the key to the Union left on Cemetery Ridge. A Union brigade headed by the 20th Maine raced to the crest just in time to stop the Confederates. As two Alabama regiments advanced on Little Round Top, the 20th Maine deployed behind trees, rocks and underbrush to meet the onslaught. After a vicious fight lasting more than an hour, the 20th Maine was almost out of ammunition and about to be overrun. With few other options, Chamberlain ordered a bayonet charge, which so shocked to exhausted Confederate attackers that they surrendered within a few minutes. After his remarkable exploits at Gettysburg, Chamberlain wen on to become one of the best--and most often wounded--brigade and division commanders in the Army of the Potomac.
