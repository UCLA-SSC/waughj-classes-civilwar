THE SOLDIER'S HISTORY OF THE CIVIL WAR

History 197I is an upper division seminar designed to introduce the techniques of historical research and analysis to advanced history majors. In this seminar, students will study Civil War soldiers and produce a paper based upon both original documents and secondary sources. Attendance and participation are essential to the success of this seminar. Students are required to attend every session -- an unexplained absence will result in a lowered grade. If you are unable to attend, (due to family emergency or illness) it is your responsibility to contact the instructor. Students are also required to read every assignment before the class meeting in order to facilitate 'discussion for that week. Finally, please be on time for the discussion section: latecomers will be downgraded.

ASSIGNMENTS

History 1971 provides the student with the opportunity to learn about the lives of ordinary, volunteer soldiers, from the North and South, who fought against each other in the American Civil War 1861-1865. To this end, the reading assignments and the class discussions will focus the student's attention on three questions of Civil War soldierhood:

Why did they enlist?
Why did they fight?
Why did they stay?

In other words, the class will examine the psychological motivations, the political and social ideals, the economic and cultural imperatives which drove a whole generation of young men to participate in the bloodiest and most dramatic conflict in American history.

The animating factors behind the soldiers' wartime experiences will also be studied through the research and writing of a TEN page paper. Students are required to select a particular fighting unit of the Civil War (regiment is recommended) and write its history. The students' choice will be made in close consultation with the Professor early in the quarter. In office hours and in seminar, the Professor and the students will discuss the selection of the paper topic. A few examples:

    How to choose the appropriate type of unit

  • (i.e. Northern or Southern?; Eastern or Western front?;
  • ethnic or racial designation?-- Irish, German, Jewish, Indian; urban or rural, working, middle or upper-class; famous or obscure -- New York's 69th; 20th Maine, 54th Massachusetts; Virginia's First Brigade, and so on--;

    How to use the available, printed original and secondary sources to advantage

  • (i.e. diaries, letters, official documents, reports, newspapers, memoirs, biographies, company, regimental, brigade histories, general background sources, music, photographs, maps.)

    How to develop a strong point-of-view

  • This means an interpretative position, which simply is an argument which gives meaning and direction to the collection of data. (i.e. A widely held belief about Northern Civil War soldiers is that they were non-political, even apathetic about the great "cause" of saving the Union. In my paper on the 20th Maine, I will demonstrate that this assertion must be qualified greatly through examining the....and so on.)
