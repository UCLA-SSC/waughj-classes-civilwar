A history paper must have three elements:
  1. An argument (thesis/point of view/interpretation),
  2. Context (making sense of your topic/putting it in terms of the history around it), and
  3. A solid and varied source base

For this paper you need to:
  1. Choose the book that best reflects your interests
  2. Ask an interesting question that you can turn into an argument
  3. Select documents and materials from the website to supplement your topic paper; also consider using parts of one or two books from my recommended lists

Here is an example of what I mean by selecting a paper topic. I don't want to use one of the assigned books for obvious reasons, so let's say that the assignment is on nine books that I could choose about Reconstruction. After careful consideration about what interests me, I select Eric Foner's Reconstruction.I like this book because it is a political/social history of the period which highlights a group I am particularly interested in exploring: scalawags. "Scalawags" (a pejorative name) were southerners who after the war joined the Republican Party in their respective states. History has not been kind to the scalawags, who were considered "traitors" to the South, and were certainly hated by the Southern Democratic Party.

My paper topic question is this: What was the role of the scalawag in the Reconstruction Era? My argument, based on Foner's book, one or two outside sources, and documents, biographies, and other information from the 147A "Reconstruction Gallery" is that, contrary to their previous historical reputation, scalawags such as General James Longstreet of Louisiana, Gov. James. L. Alcorn of Mississippi and Gov. William G. Brownlow of Tennessee represented a significant portion of the Southern population who were Unionist before the war, offered little support for the CSA during and the war, and wanted to join hands with the North after the war to remake the South into a more economically viable, progressive section. Although they failed in their aims, the scalawags' contribution to the rebuilding of the South offers many lessons to the student of Reconstruction.

What are the Structural Requirements of the Assignment?:
  1. No less than 8 full pages.
  2. No more than 10 pages.
  3. In 12 point Times Roman type.
  4. You must have normal margins of 1.25 inches on both sides and 1 inch on the top and bottom of the page.
  5. Double-spaced.
  6. You must have a title page that lists:
      a. Your name
      b. Your student I.D. number
      c. The date
      d. The book you chose to work with
    Do not put this information on the text pages of your paper.
  7. Numbered pages. If your computer will not paginate, then printing the numbers neatly is acceptable.
  8. Stapled. No report folders.
  9. Footnotes or endnotes and a bibliography
      a. The notes should follow the following format:
        Book:
          Author name. Underlined Title of Book. (city where book was published: publisher of book, year of publication), Page number of citation.

          Example: Mark E. Neely. The Fate of Liberty: Abraham Lincoln and Civil Liberties. (NY: Oxford University Press, 1992), 48.

        Article/Document in book:
          Author of article name. "Title of Article." in Underlined Title of Volume Article Appears in, ed. Name of editor of volume. (City of volume publication: publisher, date of publication), Page number of citation.

          Example: Roger Taney. "Opinion of the Court in Dred Scott, Plantiff in Error v. John F. Sanford." in Dred Scott v. Sanford: A Brief History with Documents, ed. Paul Finkelman. (Boston: St. Martins Press, 1997), 55.

        Web source:
          Author of source (if given), "Title of Source," Location in 147a gallery/site.

          Example: James Russell Lowell, "The Question of the Hour," Primary Documents: Causes of the Civil War Gallery.

      b. The bibliography should follow the above format with the following changes:
      1. Author's names should be listed last name, first name
      2. Instead of the page number of a particular citation, the bibliographic citation should end with the total number of pages in the volume.

Things to keep in mind:

CITATIONS ARE ESSENTIAL TO A PROPER PAPER. BE SURE TO CITE SOURCES WHEN APPROPRIATE. Further assistance on citations and bibliographic notes can be found in the UCLA English Department's Style Sheet which is available in the textbook section of the student store.

Proofread your paper!!! Misspellings, misused words, run-ons, and sentence fragments make papers look sloppy and imply that the writer did not care enough about their essay (and therefore their grade) to take the time to proof it. While this is not an English class, an effective paper in any course must be written coherently.
