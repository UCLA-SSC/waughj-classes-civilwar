Questions

1. An historian wrote that "An examination of the... Ieading... generals can yield an appreciation of the way each side fought the Civil War.'' How is that assertion reflected in the careers of McClellan and Lee?

2. Historian James McPherson says this: "The Civil War was pre-eminently a political war, a war of peoples rather than of professional armies." Analyze his statement in light of one or two of the following: war aims and strategies, the recruitment and enlistment of soldiers (both enlisted and officers), the slavery issue, Bull Run, The Seven Days' Battles, and Antietam.

3. Historian Reid Mitchell contends that the "cause of the union or of the South was boundup with one's community, one's home and family, and one's God... that was why the Civil War volunteer not only submitted to his transformation into a soldier, but took pride in it." How did the Civil War volunteer become a "soldier"? What events, experiences, issues, people, etc. changed him? 4. Paludan argues that "[b]efore theconflict began, Lincoln had begun emancipating the Republic" but men like Wendell Phillips disagreed with Loncoln's conduct of the War. Evaluate Lincoln's record as a leader during the buildup to and the early years of the War.
