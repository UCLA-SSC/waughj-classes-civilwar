Quiz Scoring and Answers


Scoring
  • There were three multiple choice questions on each test, worth one point each for a total of three points.
  • There were two fill in questions on each test, worth two points each for a total of four points
  • There was one matching question on each test, worth three points. To get credit for the question, all five matches had to be correct.
  • There were three terms on each test, of which you had to answer two, worth three points each, for a total of six points. Each term has two scores next to it; identifying the term itself was worth one point, identifying its significance was worth two points. So, a completely correct term will have a score 1/2 next to it, and so forth.
  • This adds up to a total of sixteen points. The test was only worth fifteen, so those who got everything right will receive one point of extra credit.

Answers

Multiple Choice
    Which of the following best describes "she-devils"?
    Southern women who harassed and spat upon occupying Union troops in various Southern cities. Typically, Union commanders were only able to resolve the problem with threats of force.


    Who is Charles Harvey Brewster?
    A clerk from Northhampton, Massachusetts who joined the 10th Massachusetts Infantry early in the war. As part of the Army of the Potomac, his unit participated in every major battle in the Eastern Theater. His letters home reveal the different emotional reactions a soldier had to the experience of battle. In 1864, he resigned from the army to take a position as a recruiter of black soldiers in Virginia.

    Note: This question was based on the assigned readings. Since there is a point of extra credit, you would still get a perfect score if you got this wrong and everything else right.


    Which of the following is NOT TRUE of the Emancipation Proclamation?
    The Proclamation immediately freed over four million slaves. Knowing that this would be a politically unpopular development among certain factions of the Union population, namely Democrats, Lincoln strove to make the proclamation as eloquent as possible, touching on themes of liberty and sacrifice. It is generally regarded as among the finest examples of Lincolnian prose.

    Note: The Proclamation did not immediately free any slaves, as it applied only to areas under Confederate control, which were not subject to Lincoln's orders. The second phrase essentially describes the Gettysburg Address; when Lincoln wrote the Emancipation Proclamation he deliberately made it as dry and legalistic as he could.

Matching
    George B. McClellan -> Molded the Army of the Potomac into a fighting machine. His staunchly Democratic views brought him into frequent conflict with President Lincoln, however, and resulted in his being removed from command, then reinstated and then removed again.

    George Gordon Meade -> Named to command the army of the Potomac on the eve of the battle of Gettysburg, he managed to come away with a victory, and became a hero in the North.

    Irvin McDowell -> The first commander of the Army of the Potomac to be sacked, after the Union's loss at First Bull Run.

    Joseph Hooker -> A hard-fighting, hard-drinking general, he commanded the Army of the Potomac for only one battle, Chancellorsville, before resigning in May of 1863.

    Ambrose Burnside -> Named to command the Army of the Potomac in the latter part of 1862, he was not up to the task, and resigned after the "Mud March" and the battle of Fredericksburg, both debacles.



    Robert E. Lee -> Took responsibility for the Confederate defeat, and even tendered his resignation, which was declined.

    James Longstreet -> His hesitation in attacking at Gettysburg has been identified by many historians as the cause of the Confederate defeat there.

    Thomas J. "Stonewall" Jackson -> Perhaps the South's best fighting general, it has been suggested that the South would have won the battle if he had been present.

    J. E. B. Stuart -> His cavalry troops were the "eyes" of the Army of the Virginia. He failed in his responsibilities, however, at Gettysburg.

    George Pickett -> The ill-fated charge made by his troops on the third day of the battle was a bloody disaster, bringing an end to the battle and assuring victory for the North.



Terms
    Robert Gould Shaw/Fort Wagner: Robert Gould Shaw was the commander of the 54th Massachusetts colored infantry regiment, which demonstrated its capacity to fight at the Battle of Fort Wagner in July of 1863. The use of black troops affirmed that a Union victory would revolutionize the status of African-Americans, as the black men who had worn the blue would have a powerful claim to the rights and liberties guaranteed by the Constitution and the Declaration of Independence.

    Rifled Musket/Massed Infantry Assault: Rifled muskets have grooves inside their barrel, increasing the guns' firing range and thus making them much more deadly than non-rifled muskets, particularly against the traditional massed infantry assault that had been the basis of warfare in the Western world for centuries. The slaughter that resulted from massed assaults against troops armed with rifles eventually forced generals to change the way they made war, presaging the "modern warfare" of the 20th century.

    Clara Barton/U.S. Sanitary Commission: The U.S. Sanitary Commission sent medical supplies and nurses (including Clara Barton) to aid hurt and sick Union soldiers. The nurses, along with many other women during the Civil War, were assuming responsibilities that had traditionally been off-limits to women, thus building the foundation for a radical redefinition of gender roles over the course of the next several generations.
