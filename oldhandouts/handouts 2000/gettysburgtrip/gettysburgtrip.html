UCLA's Department of History invites you to ponder the Battle of Gettysburg -- Monday, June 25 - Monday, August 6, 2001*

The battle of Gettysburg, fought on July 1-3, 1863, is considered by many to be the "turning point" of the American Civil War. Today, Gettysburg, Pennsylvania is the destination for over two million tourists a year. In no other place in America is history so interwoven with the fabric of daily life. From the beautiful buildings of Gettysburg College, through the historically preserved streets and houses of the town, to the magnificent national military park, the student of American history finds an incomparable intellectual and sensory experience.

The course offers students an opportunity to study intensively the political, social, economic, cultural, and military history of Gettysburg, first through lectures, discussions, and research on the UCLA campus, and then on a field trip to Gettysburg where students will visit the town, the college, and the battlefield in-depth. Guest lecturers, archival work, and on-site visits led by specialists are planned to enhance and enrich students' ability to write a serious research paper on Gettysburg's Civil War history. Two days of touring Civil War sites around Washington, D.C. are planned, including trips to Harper's Ferry and Fredericksburg, Virginia.

Directed by Professor Joan Waugh, Department of History, UCLA. For more information, please visit www.summer.ucla.edu/travel.
