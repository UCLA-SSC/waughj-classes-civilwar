Term Sample 4

Freedmen's Bureau: Created in 1865, to dispense rations and relief to the hundreds of thousands of blacks and whites who had been displaced by the war. The bureau also had the power to distribute forty acre parcels of "abandoned land". Whether they could do this was questionable, but in any case, the Freedmen's Bureau represented an unprecedented extension of federal authority into the social sphere-to meet the unprecedented demands of dealing with 4,000,000 uprooted slaves and create Southern society anew.

The bureau was directed by General Oliver Howard, who was competent, but unpopular with white Southerners. Several common criticisms:

- meddled in affairs not under the auspices of the government
- stirred up discontentment among African Americans and filled them with false hopes
- incompetent or corrupt administrators
- wasted money
- acting as a political agency of the Republican party
These criticisms are a little too harsh, though. The Bureau had some constructive achievements, especially considering how underfunded it was and how overwhelming the task it faced was. The Bureau's successes:
- Provided desperately needed emergency relief
- Built as many schools for blacks as was possible
- Tried to prevent landowners from taking advantage of African Americans. It set aside some of the black codes, and supervised the making and enforcement of labor contracts
- Tried to protect the Civil Rights of blacks, particularly through the establishment of special black courts
By 1869, the Freedmen's Bureau was terminated, thus depriving Congress of its best means for protecting blacks, and enforcing the newly passed Civil Rights amendments.
