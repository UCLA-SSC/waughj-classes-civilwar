The African Americans that fought for the Union during the Civil War faced many conflicting ideals. They wished to uphold the Union. However, this Union was prejudiced and separatist. These colored volunteers wished to prove their manhood and promote the ideals of freedom and equality. By serving in the Union army, the African American volunteers hoped to reflect their capacity for citizenship and legal equality. Thus, the Massachusetts 54th Volunteer Infantry Regiment's significance lay in its symbolic nature.

The movie, Glory, depicts the experiences of the Massachusetts 54th Volunteer Infantry Regiment. This depiction, however, is not historically accurate due to omission of Sgt. William A. Carney. Carney's character was replaced with a composite character named Trip, portrayed by Denzel Washington. The choice to substitute Trip for Carney affects the symbolism of the film. Prior to the charge on Fort Wagner, Colonel Shaw, played by Matthew Broderick, approaches Trip and asks him to carry the Union colors in the battle. Trip refuses. As an ex-slave, Trip has been hardened by his experiences with racism and prejudice. However, during the battle, the flag bearer is killed and Trip assumes the responsibility of carrying the flag. This is a symbolic gesture, as Trip comes to accept the Union and the love of his country. He is willing to die for his country, which is the ultimate sacrifice. Thus, Trip is killed, carrying the Union flag.

Trip's character accurately portrays the sentiments of many African Americans during the Civil War and Reconstruction era. The Emancipation Proclamation admitted the African Americans into the Union to fight for their country. This was a major step toward black citizenship. However, the importance of the Emancipation Proclamation lay in its symbolic nature. The Emancipation Proclamation did not change the status of the African American in the Union. African Americans still did not have the right to vote or serve on juries. Thus, African Americans that fought wondered what lay in store for them I after the Civil War. Trip's character accurately exemplifies this sentiment. In addition, Trip treats Thomas, a friend of Colonel Shaw's, with much disdain. Trip sees Thomas as an "Uncle Tom", or an African American who always defers to whites. This separatist attitude was prevalent throughout the African American population during the Civil War. The ex-slaves had difficulties accepting the intermingling of the two races that took place in the North. Racial tension and prejudice did exist in the North. This is exemplified by the salary discrimination. African American soldiers received $3.00 dollars less than white soldiers during the Civil War. This overt display of racial discrimination infuriated the African American soldiers, and exemplified the Union's racist attitudes. Thus, Trip's questioning of Union intentions was justified.

Thus, the Trip's character accurately portrays many of the tensions that existed within African American soldiers during the Civil War. Trip resolves these tensions by the end of the movie, as illustrated by his acceptance of the flag bearing responsibility. As Trip grabs the flag, he is mortally wounded. This symbolic gesture is the cornerstone of the film, Glory, and is essential to the story of the 54th Massachusetts.
