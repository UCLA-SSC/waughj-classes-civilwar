The movie, Glory, gives an historical account of the Civil War, but it also incorporates some fictional elements to recreate the story of the many nameless black soldiers in the war. By replacing Sgt. William A. Carney with a fictional character, Trip, the movie becomes a more symbolic story, representing the 180,000 black soldiers during the Civil War.

Trip is an embodiment of the sufferings and hardships that African-Americans experienced during the period. The fact that he was a run-away ex-slave, brings him into connection with the majority of the African-Americans during the time. Having been only exploited and abused previously, he has a hard time getting out of the "niger" mentality. For instance, Trip is extremely hostile to Thomas Searles because he thinks that Thomas is trying to emulate white people. He is bitterly sarcastic toward Thomas's "white" accent, sophisticated mannerism, and education. Unlike Thomas who enjoyed the fortunes of being born a free man in a Northern state, Trip is uneducated, crude, and deprived.

The Massachusetts 54th Regiment was recruited from the Northern states, and the volunteered blacks were mostly free and literate. However, the movie intentionally focuses on the ex-slave character, Trip, to bring the audience into a bigger perspective. As James McPherson says, the Glory is a story "simply not about the Fifty-fourth Massachusetts but about the blacks in the Civil War". Though the movie may have skewed away from recreating the actual persons from the 54th, it is a more fair representation of the black soldiers in the war. The black regiments formed after the 54th were made up of 178,000 men, most of whom had just recently been freed from slavery.

Though it first began as a fight to save the Union, the Civil War later became a war to end the slavery. Sgt. Carney probably was more educated and had a better self-image than Trip did. As a Northern black, he most likely had a more clear ideas about why he was fighting the war. Carney may have fought for racial equality or equal citizenship. However, with Trip, the story has more transitions. The Glory is a story about a man's inner transformation. In the beginning of the movie, Trip is full of fear and insecurity (shown by how he uses the word, "niger", and intimidates others to protect himself). But he changes into a brave and courageous soldier who finds his self-worth and importance through his experience in the regiment. When given a chance to fight and had been trusted by his Colonel, Trip shows an extraordinary level of development and strength.

Perhaps the movie loses the accurate historicity of the 54th Regiment by adding in fictional characters. However, by making issue out of slavery through a character like Trip, the movie defines the historical meaning of Civil War in the American history. Slavery is the reason why the war was fought, and the fact that these ex-slaves had corporately fought for the Union army's cause has a great significance.

The song, "Give us a Flag", has a part that says, "So rally, boys, rally, let us never mind the past/We had a hard road to travel, but our day is coming fast". The song encourages the black soldiers to overcome the slavery experience and says that a different world is approaching for them, as their "day is coming fast". Trip is exactly the character that overcomes such difficulty and accepts a more hopeful view of the future. Trip gives the story of one black man's triumph and transformation.
